**Brand Guidelines**
 
# **Introduction**
 
This document defines the visual identity and brand standards for Martin Blow. It covers colour, typography, tone of voice, and application across Word documents, PowerPoint presentations, and social media content. These guidelines ensure consistency across all professional materials.
 
The brand reflects the values that underpin the work: clarity, credibility, and a grounded, considered approach to complex problems in data and AI leadership.
 
# **Colour Palette**
 
The colour palette is built around deep navy as the primary brand colour, supported by a mid blue secondary, a lighter accent blue for highlights, a warm slate neutral, and a warm white background tone. Together these create a professional, authoritative feel suited to senior financial services audiences.

### **Colour Usage Principles**
 
- Navy (#0C447C) should be used for all primary elements: document headers, slide backgrounds, key shapes, and primary headings.
 
- Mid Blue (#185FA5) is used for secondary headings and supporting structural elements.
 
- Accent Blue (#378ADD) should be used sparingly for highlights, divider lines, callouts, and interactive elements. It draws the eye and should not compete with the primary navy.
 
- Slate (#5F5E5A) is the body text and caption colour. It is softer than black and reads well on warm white backgrounds.
 
- Warm White (#F1EFE8) is the default background for documents and light slides. It is warmer than pure white and reduces visual fatigue.
 
# **Typography**
 
The brand uses Arial throughout for its universal availability, clean legibility, and professional neutrality. It avoids the decorative quality of serif fonts while remaining more approachable than purely technical alternatives.

# **Tone of Voice**
 
All written content — posts, documents, presentations — should reflect the following principles:
 
## **Thoughtful and Measured**
 
Share considered perspectives from experience, not hot takes or manufactured controversy. Readers should feel they are hearing from someone who has genuinely been in the room. Avoid proclamations. Make observations.
 
## **Human and Personal**
 
Write as a practitioner, not as a content creator. The voice should be intelligent without being academic, and personal without being casual. Avoid AI-sounding phrasing, formulaic structures, and overused LinkedIn tropes.
 
## **Grounded in Evidence**
 
Where possible, ground observations in specific experience, real numbers, or named sources. Abstract claims without evidence read as generic. Specific proof points — even briefly referenced — establish credibility.
 
## **What to Avoid**
 
- Em dashes used as a stylistic device.
 
- The construction: 'It isn't X, it is Y' — rephrase to sound more natural.
 
- Manufactured hooks or controversial angles designed to provoke engagement.
 
- Closing questions that feel added on rather than genuinely inviting reflection.
 
- One-line-per-paragraph formatting common on LinkedIn.
 
- Excessive use of bold, bullet walls, or emoji.
 
# **Content Topics**
 
LinkedIn content is organised around eight core topic areas, each reflecting genuine expertise and positioned for a senior financial services audience:
 
- Data strategy and commercial value — translating data investment into measurable business outcomes.
 
- AI and GenAI in data workflows — practical, grounded observations on AI in enterprise data contexts.
 
- Data products in financial services — designing for reuse, not just delivery.
 
- Leadership and operating models — building data teams and aligning senior stakeholders.
 
- The consultant and interim perspective — observations from moving between organisations.
 
- Financial services data challenges — cloud modernisation, analytics in regulated environments.
 
- Data governance as an enabler — governance as a foundation for value, not overhead.
 
- Broader technology and society — connecting technology trends to wider human questions.
 
# **Document Application**
 
## **Word Documents**
 
All Word documents use the Martin Blow Word Template. Key conventions:
 
- Header: Name and document type in navy, with an accent blue bottom border.
 
- Footer: Contact details and page number in slate, with an accent blue top border.
 
- H1 headings use navy with an accent blue underline border.
 
- H2 headings use mid blue. H3 headings use accent blue.
 
- Body text is slate on a warm white background.
 
- Tables use navy header rows with white text and light grey borders.
 
## **PowerPoint Presentations**
 
All presentations use the Martin Blow PowerPoint Template, which includes five slide layouts:
 
- Title slide — dark navy background, white headline, accent blue divider line.
 
- Content slide — warm white background, navy header bar, left accent bar.
 
- Stat callout slide — three navy boxes with large accent-coloured numbers.
 
- Section divider — mid blue background, white headline, accent blue rule.
 
- Closing slide — dark navy background matching the title slide.
 
## **Social Media**
 
LinkedIn post graphics follow the brand palette. Images should be visually creative and concept-led rather than text-heavy. Minimal headline text in bold white sans-serif on a navy background. No name on post graphics. No logo required at this stage.
 
# **LinkedIn Profile**
 
## **Headline**
 
The LinkedIn headline should be a positioning statement, not a job title. Recommended: 'Data and AI leader | Turning data into commercial value for financial services'.
 
## **Posting Frequency**
 
Target two posts per week, scheduled through Buffer. Posts should be 80 to 130 words as a default. Posts grounded in a specific topic may run to 180 words where the additional length is earned.
 
## **Post Format**
 
Clean prose is the default format. Numbered lists or bullets only where they genuinely serve the content, not as a structural crutch. No emoji. No hashtag blocks. No manufactured opening hooks.
